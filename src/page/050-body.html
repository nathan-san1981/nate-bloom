DJ · how it works

Load a song on each deck (a file, or anything already in a sampler slot or the bank). The tempo (BPM) and the beat grid are found automatically. If the BPM looks doubled or halved, use ½× / 2×; if it's wrong, tap Tap along with 4+ beats.

AUTO SYNC matches the other song's tempo to the lead song and lines up the beats, starting it if it's stopped. While it's lit, the songs stay locked: change the lead's tempo and the other follows.

By hand: the sync dial sets the other song's tempo (slow turns are fine, fast turns are big moves; it clicks into place when the tempos match). The ring inside shows the beats: dot at the top and green = in time. Hold ◀◀ slow or ▶▶ push to drag the other song back or forward into place, or jump a whole beat. The strip on top shows both songs' beat lines; stacked lines mean they're in time.

Decks: the big number is the song's BPM as it plays. Speed (the slider, − / +, tap the % to reset) changes how fast it plays. ½× 2× Tap only fix the BPM number when it was found wrong (double, half, or no beat): tap Tap four or more times along with the beat. Tap the wave to jump anywhere; Cue works like a CDJ: stopped, tap it to set the cue point (it snaps to the beat; a new song's cue starts on its first beat), hold it to hear the song from the cue until you let go (press ▶ while holding to keep playing); playing, Cue goes back to the cue point and stops, ready to drop. ⏮ restarts the song from its first beat. The four coloured hot cues each remember a spot: tap an empty one to set it where the song is; tap a set one to jump there (with Q on, a playing deck jumps on the next beat so the rhythm carries on; a stopped deck starts from it). To clear one, hold it, or tap ✕ at the end of the row and then the cue; hold ✕ to clear all four. Hot cues only ever move their own deck: with sync on, the jump lands on the other song's beat and the other song carries on untouched. Loop beats 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 or 32 loops that many beats from the beat the deck is on (tap it again to play on); a hot cue tapped inside the loop keeps it, one outside it ends it. Song loops the whole song on that deck: from its first beat to the end of its last whole bar, so it goes round on the beat and stays locked with the other deck (the intro plays only the first time). Tap Song again to let the song end; on a keyboard, E does it for A and I for B. Auto DJ waits while a deck loops its song. ▶ Both starts both decks at the same moment (the other song comes in on the lead's beat) and stops both when both play; ⏮ Both starts both from their first beats together. With Link on, starting a deck (⏮, ▶ or a hot cue) within 1.5 seconds of the other joins it as if you had pressed both at once, so two quick restarts land together. Loop 1 2 4 8 loops that many beats. High / Mid / Low pull a band out (all the way left kills it), Filter sweeps low-pass (left) or high-pass (right).

Sample bank: choose where to grab from and how many beats, then tap an empty pad: it captures exactly that many beats, in time, from the next beat (Mix grabs both decks through the crossfader and EQ). Tap a full pad to play it; with In time on it starts on the beat at the current tempo. Hold a pad to send it to the sampler or the pads, put it on a deck, save a WAV, or clear it. The bank and the decks are kept on this device.

Phrase on a deck marks where a musical phrase starts (the first beat of a chorus): mixes into that deck then wait for its next 8-bar line and the next song starts from its own phrase mark; without one, mixes start on the next bar line.

Key lock keeps a song's pitch when its speed changes (turn it off for the vinyl-style pitch shift). Keep saves the last 90 seconds you heard, after the fact — no need to press Rec first — and the take bar can export it as WAV or a small MP3.

✦ Smart mix reads both songs (the coloured strip under each waveform shows their sections: intro, build-up, drop, breakdown, groove, outro) and plans the mix into the song on the other deck, or the next one from Songs: where to leave the playing song (usually its outro or a breakdown), where the next one comes in (its first downbeat or Phrase mark, so its first drop lands as the mix ends), the tempo they meet at, the style and its length, and a key shift when that makes the keys fit. The plan says why; pick another spot, style or key there, then Start the mix. The next song comes in exactly when the playing one gets there: MIX IN counts down (tap it to go now) and ✦ cancels. While it gets ready (loading the next song, reading both), the progress bar at the top shows each step and about how long is left; ✕ there cancels. It can't hear vocals, so two vocals over each other are still up to your ears.

Key on each deck moves the song up or down in semitones without changing its speed (it uses key lock); tap the number to put it back. Next to it are the song's key and Camelot code, and where a shift takes it.

→ Tracks puts both songs on Tracks at one tempo (a whole BPM; the app's tempo is set to match so the grid lines up), with key lock and any key shift, lined up on the beat: as you hear them if both decks are playing, otherwise with their first downbeats on the same bar. If the keys clash and neither deck is shifted, the second song is moved to fit. Then split, copy and move sections to build a mashup.

The decks keep playing when you press Done, so you can play the keys over the mix. ■ Stop here stops the DJ (decks, pads, the play-along beat, a mix in progress); the red ■ All, or ■ Stop all on the main screen, stops everything. The main screen's own Stop leaves the DJ playing.

Beat Pads

Pads. 16 pads, 4 banks. Pad 1 is bottom-left, like an MPC. Bank A is the Studio kit (real drums) laid out for finger drumming, bank B is Studio percussion, C and D are for your chops. Clear at the end of the bank row empties every pad on that bank (and its hits in this sequence); Undo brings them back. Hit higher on a pad for louder, lower for softer; Full level makes every hit full.

Kits. Kit… in the bank row loads a whole kit onto the bank you're on: the Studio drums and percussion, 808, real 808 samples, 909, Trap, FM & FX, hand percussion and the library kits. ▶ plays a quick groove on a kit first. Every kit sits the same way (kick, snare and hats on the bottom row, rim and clap above, then toms, cymbals on top), so a beat you recorded plays on the new kit, and Undo puts the old pads back.

Keyboard. The pads are the left-hand block of keys, laid out like the pads: 1 2 3 4 are the top row (pads 13–16), Q W E R 9–12, A S D F 5–8 and Z X C V the bottom row (pads 1–4). Each pad shows its key. With the right hand: Space plays or stops, Enter records, ← → change the bank, ↑ ↓ the sequence, Esc closes Beat Pads (it keeps playing), Ctrl Z (⌘ Z on a Mac) takes back the last recording pass, and holding Shift turns on Repeat while you hold pads. The keys show on computers and whenever you use a keyboard; Letters on keys in the menu turns them off.

Recording. Pick the loop length and tap ● Rec: one bar of count-in, then everything you hit goes into the loop, and each time round you can add more (overdub). ↶ Undo takes back the last pass. Quantize moves hits onto the grid (Strength sets how far) and Swing pushes the off-beats late, MPC style; both work on what's already recorded, so you can change them after.

Repeat. Turn on Repeat, pick a rate, and hold a pad for a roll in time. Move your finger up or down the pad to change how hard it hits.

A pad. Tap a pad to pick it: its sound (any Rhythm Creator sound, a sampler slot or a chop), Level, Tune, Decay, Tone, Pan, a choke group (pads in the same group cut each other off, like a closed hat stopping an open one), Reverse for chops, and Erase to remove that pad's hits from the loop.

Chop. Pick a sampler slot or load a file, choose Transients (cuts where hits start; Sensitivity finds more or fewer) or equal parts, choose a bank and tap Chop to pads: slice 1 goes on pad 1, and so on. Tap the waveform to hear a slice.

Sequences. Eight of them, each with its own loop length and hits. Tap a number to switch; while playing, the switch happens at the end of the loop (the number blinks until then). Copy puts the current sequence into the next empty one and switches to it, for a variation or a fill.

Song. Turn on Song and + Seq adds the sequence you're on to the chain. Tap a step to play it ×1 to ×4, ✕ removes it. Play runs the chain from the top and loops it. Recording turns Song off and records into the sequence you're on.

16 Levels. Pick a pad and turn on 16 Levels: all 16 pads play that sound, from pad 1 to pad 16 rising in Tune (pad 9 is as recorded), Velocity, Decay or Filter. Recorded hits keep their level, so a bassline played from one 808 boom plays back as played.

Resample renders the sequence (or the song when Song is on) into a free sampler slot and the next empty pad, ready to chop. A sequence comes out as a seamless loop. FX aren't printed in, so turn on FX to hear them on the new pad. → Tracks puts it on a new track in Tracks as clips: the sequence four times, or one clip per song step, to rearrange there.

MIDI. With Beat Pads open, a pad controller or the K.O. II plays the pads (notes 36–51 are pads 1–16, others wrap round), and incoming Start/Stop runs Beat Pads. A MiniLab 3's pads 1–8 play pads 1–8 (the bottom two rows), its bank B pads 9–16; its knobs edit the pad you hit last, and its keys play that pad in tune. With Send clock on in the MIDI sheet, Beat Pads and the Rhythm Creator send clock and start/stop too.

Timing. The tempo is the app's (the Rhythm Creator's, and a playing DJ deck's when Auto BPM is on). When the Rhythm Creator is playing, Beat Pads starts on its next bar. FX sends the pads through the effects and FX Lab; the Mixer has a Beat Pads fader.

Rhythm Creator

Tracks. Sixteen tracks, one sound each. Tap a track's name to select it and hear it; its sound and knobs appear under the grid. Mute and Solo are there too.

Sounds. 808 (synthesized from the original circuit), 909, Analog (modelled kick, snare and hats), FM, Trap (808 boom with pitch glide, snap, stacked clap, trap hat), Hand percussion, Electro & FX (zap, noise riser and reverse cymbal that last a number of beats set by Decay, tom drop), Real 808 samples, and your sampler slots, pads and kits.

Knobs. Level, Pan, Tune (±24 semitones), Decay, Tone, Character (its name changes with the sound: Snappy, Click, Glide…), a filter (low-pass, high-pass or band-pass with frequency and resonance), Drive, and the track's own length. A shorter length loops sooner than the others: 12 steps against 16 makes a polyrhythm.

Steps. The row above the grid sets what a tap does. Hits turns steps on and off. Accent plays louder (yellow). Flam adds a soft grace hit just before. Roll cycles 2, 3 and 4 hits inside the step. Chance cycles 75%, 50%, 25% and bar conditions: 1:2 plays on the first of every two loops, 2:2 on the second, 1:4 and 4:4 likewise. Nudge moves the hit a quarter or half step late or early.

Real drums. The Studio kit is a recorded acoustic kit: every hit has up to four loudness layers (how hard you play picks the layer) and alternate takes, so two hits in a row are never the same recording. Studio percussion is recorded too. Kit swaps every track to the matching sound of a kit and keeps the pattern (Undo puts it back). Feel is how human it plays: small timing and loudness differences, hats that breathe, off-beat snare hits as ghost notes; 0 is machine-tight. Room is how much of the studio room you hear; on a Studio sound, Character is that track's own room amount.

Euclid. Under the knobs: spreads a number of hits as evenly as possible over the track's length; Rotate shifts the result.

Make a beat. Pick a style, how busy and how complex, and tap Make: every track gets a part that fits its sound, and the tempo and swing are set for the style. Vary changes about 15% of the hits and keeps the downbeats.

Morph. Save A, change the knobs, Save B, then slide between the two kits.

Timing. Tempo is shared with the sequencer, and when the sequencer is playing the Rhythm Creator locks to it. DJ next to the tempo is Auto BPM (on by default): while a DJ deck plays, the Rhythm Creator takes that deck's tempo and locks to its beat, with step 1 on the song's bar lines (or on its Phrase mark), and stays with it through speed changes, sync and hot cue jumps. When the deck stops it keeps that tempo until you stop. Tap − or + to take the tempo back (Auto turns off; Undo turns it on again). Closed hats cut off open hats. Steps sets the pattern length (16 to 64, in pages).

Sound. FX → Apply to → Rhythm sends it through the effects. Mix has a Rhythm Creator fader. ● Rec records everything you hear.

808 voices follow iO-808 by Vincent Riemer (MIT licence). Analog models are ported from Mutable Instruments Plaits by Emilie Gillet (MIT licence). Real 808 samples: Michael Fischer's TR-808 set (CC0). Studio kit and Studio percussion: Virtuosity Drums by Versilian Studios, played by Austin McMahon (CC0).

K.O. II setup

Wiring. Hub in the tablet, the charger in the hub's USB-C PD port, the K.O. II in a USB-A port with a data cable (a charge-only cable won't show up here).

Sound A (best). The hub's 3.5 mm jack into the K.O. II's IN; listen on the K.O. II's OUT. One mix, one volume knob, and the K.O. II's FX work on Nate Bloom. Tablet volume around 70–80% so its input doesn't distort.

Sound B (K.O. II OS 2.5+). The USB cable carries the audio too. On the K.O. II: SHIFT+ERASE, 5 1 1 ENTER to hear it live; its sampler can take USB as the source to sample Nate Bloom. Android uses one USB audio device at a time, usually the last one plugged in.

Nate Bloom leads (recommended). Tick Send clock and start/stop; on the K.O. II: SHIFT+ERASE, 1 0 1 ENTER. Play here (sequencer, Rhythm Creator or Beat Pads) starts the K.O. II at this tempo.

K.O. II leads. On the K.O. II: SHIFT+ERASE, 1 0 2 ENTER; tick Follow incoming clock. Its Play starts Beat Pads or the Rhythm Creator when one is open, otherwise the sequencer. Nate Bloom then sounds late by the tablet's audio delay, which nothing can pull back.

Line up. Put a four-on-the-floor kick on both. If the K.O. II's kick comes first, slide Line up the K.O. II right; if Nate Bloom's does, slide left, until they land as one hit. Redo it when you switch between A and B.

K.O. II pads → Nate Bloom. Give a pad a MIDI channel (SHIFT+SOUND, knob X; knob Y sets the root note). Sound 000 makes a MIDI-only pad: in keys mode it plays and sequences Nate Bloom's synths. If drum pads trigger the synth, system 1 2 7 sends only from pads with a channel.

Recording the K.O. II. Over USB audio it's also an input: Samples → Record input → pick the K.O. II.

How Nate Bloom works

Getting around: Synths · Samples · Drums pick what you play: the synth engines (with Lib, Inst and your pinned sounds), the sampler and the 12 pads, or the drum machine; the row of tabs under them shows only that section's sounds. DJ, Rhythm (the Rhythm Creator), Beats (Beat Pads) and Garage open full screens of their own (a green dot means one is playing). Keys · Seq · Notes switch what this page shows. On a phone the sections sit in the bar along the bottom; on a tablet or a computer they're in the top bar. The top bar also has Mix, ● Rec, the red ■ that stops everything, ↶ (undo) and ☰, which holds the rest: Projects, Plugins, My sounds, Export, MIDI, Install, History, this help and Exit. Controls that don't apply to what you're playing (Chord, octave, Arp and Hold on the drums and pads) are hidden. Rows that scroll sideways fade at the edge where there's more. The Playing ▾ under the top bar lists everything making sound (sequencer, Rhythm Creator, DJ decks, arps, Jam, Tracks, held notes, pads). Tap it for a card per thing, with Only this (hear just that one; the rest keep going, silent), Edit (go to it) and ■ Stop. An arp keeps playing on its synth when you pick another synth; Edit takes you back to it with its notes held, to add or take away notes. What you play on the keys yourself always sounds. Back (the Android gesture) closes whatever is open on top. ↶ takes back the last change (see Undo); big changes (a new beat, Clear) also offer Undo for a few seconds. ⤢ next to the octave buttons is Play view: just the keys, bigger. Report a problem is at the top of this help.

Keys · Seq · Notes are the three views. Keys is the instrument, Seq is the step sequencer for drums and a one-bar tune, Notes is a staff you write a melody on.

Tabs across the top pick a sound, in three groups: Sounds you've put there, the 13 Engines (the synths themselves, each with its own sliders), and More: Lib (hundreds of real instruments plus Classic Synths, 30 famous sounds ready to play), Smp for your own recordings and samples (its slots S1–S8 and Load sit just under the Sound · FX · Arp row, whichever of those is open), and Drums, three drum machines you can play together.

Playing engines together: tapping an engine tab switches to it. To stack two or more, tap the + badge on the corner of another engine's tab (it turns into an amber ✓), or long-press the tab and choose "Play together". The sound bar lists what's stacked.

Jam (Sound · FX · Arp · Jam): four loops record whatever you play (keys, drums, pads, with the sound you used), quantised, up to 4 bars, and go round in time — tap a loop to record, tap again to stop, tap to mute or play, ✕ to clear. The Band plays a bass line and a beat in a style you pick, following whatever chord you hold on the keys (the chord shows in the strip). Both run at the sequencer tempo, or the DJ tempo while a deck plays.

Analog (Sound tab: Analog next to ▶ Demo opens it): the synths' filters are modelled analog circuits: a 24 dB ladder on Juno, Brass and Mono, a 12 dB state-variable filter on Supersaw, Analog, Morph, Sync and Pluck. Drive pushes them into warm saturation, Drift gives every note its own slowly wandering tuning like real oscillators, and Filter can put one model on every synth. Off goes back to the clean digital filters (Lite starts with it off to save the battery and CPU). Changes apply to the next notes you play.

MiniLab 3 (Arturia): plug it in (Chrome on the tablet or a Chromebook), MIDI → Connect, and every screen gives it a layout of its own: knobs shape what's on the screen (the sound's knobs on Synths; the track you hit on Rhythm; the pad you hit on Beats; the deck EQs on DJ; the selected track's Smart Controls in the Garage), faders mix, pads play (drums and chords in your key on Synths, the slots and pads on Samples, the 16 tracks on Grid, the 16 pads on Pads, deck A and deck B on DJ, the drums or chords of the selected Garage track) and the big knob picks: turn it for the next sound, track, pad or sequence, click it to play or stop; with Shift held, turn for tempo (or the bank, a deck's speed…) and click for ● Rec. Turn a knob and the screen says what it moved; K1–K8 and F1–F4 mark the controls; the tab at the right edge opens the map of the screen you're on. On the tablet the strip across the top shows all of them by name: drag a knob or fader there to move it by touch, tap a pad to play it, and hold one (or ✎, then tap it) to give it another job on this screen; the MiniLab follows (MIDI sheet: Strip at the top). The knobs pick up from wherever each control already is, so changing screens never makes anything jump. MIDI learn (MIDI sheet, or Learn in the map) gives a knob or fader another job on the screen you're on, and only there. On Beats the MiniLab's keys play the last pad you hit, in tune; on Rhythm, Shift + click the big knob and the pads write their hits into the grid while it plays. Oct − / Oct + move the MiniLab's own keys (it sends nothing for them), and the screen follows the notes they send: the keys, Play's Octave buttons and the strip move once a note lands outside the window they were on. Shift + pads 13–16 (loop, stop, play, record) drive ▶ ■ ● on the screen you're on.

Bitwig (the Linux app): the app puts the Nate Bloom Bridge plugin in ~/.clap. In Bitwig, put it on a track (Instruments, by Nate Bloom; rescan plugins the first time). The track's notes, knobs and wheels play a sound of Nate Bloom's, and that sound comes back on the track, ready to record, mix and add Bitwig effects. The plugin reports about 56 ms of latency, and Bitwig's delay compensation lines it up with everything else. Use one bridge per sound you want on its own track. Bitwig in the top strip opens the sheet:

  • Each bridge, named after its track, has a menu of what it carries: Everything (what's playing, the mix before Nate Bloom's master compressor) or one sound.
  • Hear its input lets audio sent into the bridge (a sidechain) play through Nate Bloom.
  • Only through Bitwig turns Nate Bloom's own output off while a bridge carries everything, and takes a bridged sound out of Nate Bloom's mix, so nothing plays twice.
  • Follow Bitwig's tempo, and optionally Start and stop with Bitwig.
For other apps there are also MIDI ports: "Nate Bloom in" plays what's playing, and "Nate Bloom send" → "Nate Bloom out" carries Nate Bloom's MIDI out. JACK ports add a stereo pair for the mix and for four parts (JACK · part N in the sheet), for Bitwig on its JACK engine or qpwgraph; they need pipewire-jack or JACK. Ableton Link keeps the tempo shared both ways. Bitwig's audio buffer should be 1024 frames or less.

Which controller plays which sound: each sound in Playing has a 🎹 menu. Any keys (the default) means every keyboard that plays what's playing. Tick one or more controllers instead and only they play it (the screen's keys and the sequencer always do). A controller ticked on a sound plays it even if it plays nothing else, so the MicroFreak's keys can play a plugin while its own sound comes through pass-through; its sheet has the same ticks under Its keys and knobs also play. Its knobs, wheels and pressure go to a plugin it plays.

Which controller plays what (MIDI sheet): each input that's on has a menu. What's playing plays the sound stack, as before. Nothing leaves it to the pads, clock and learned knobs. You can also give it a sound of its own: a synth, a library sound or plugin, the instrument, a sampler slot or the hardware synth, whatever the rest of the stack is. Several controllers can play the same sound. Each controller's sustain pedal holds its own notes. A controller that plays a plugin (a VST3, CLAP or LV2 in the Linux app) also sends it its knobs, pitch and mod wheels and pressure, so the plugin's own MIDI learn takes the MicroFreak's knobs, say. Knobs learned in Nate Bloom keep their jobs there. These notes play straight to that sound; the chord, Hold and the arp work on What's playing.

Hardware synths (MicroFreak, Minilogue XD, MiniFreak, Crave, Grind, or any synth on a MIDI output): add it from Quick sounds and Nate Bloom plays it with the rest; its sound comes back through your audio interface into its own Mixer channel. In its sheet, Which synth picks the model (Automatic finds the first one plugged in). Pass-through keeps its input open all the time, so its own presets, keys, sequencer and arp, and any pedal after it, come through whether or not Nate Bloom is playing it, and Takes, the Garage and Live's recorder have them too. Its own effects come through as they are, and it reaches the mix clean: at the level the interface gives it, past Nate Bloom's effects chain, master volume and master compressor (only the safety limiter, at −3 dBFS, is in its way), so its low end and attack stay as they are. Nate Bloom's effects on it sends it through the Effects knobs and the master like the other sounds, and plugin effects can go on its channel from the Plugins sheet. Direct monitoring is for when your interface plays it in your headphones itself (on the Volt 2, its Direct Monitoring button lit orange or blue). Nate Bloom then doesn't play it back, so you don't hear it twice. It records it raw, at full level and lined up the way you hear it, and sends its notes about 60 ms later so it meets Nate Bloom's own sounds in your ears, so recordings match your headphones. With it off, switch the Volt's Direct Monitoring button off (unlit), or you hear it twice and the low end thins out.

Sampler, in the Linux app: ▶ Play by Edit plays the selected slot without the keys (it loops when the slot's Loop is on). The Player plays any of the eight slots at once, each looping or not, at its own level and pitch. With On the bar on, a slot you start comes in on the next bar of the ones already playing, so loops layer in time. The editor adds rows of tools. View: zoom (or the mouse wheel), fit the selection, scroll, and Snap, which moves the selection's edges to zero crossings. Edit: copy, paste over the selection, insert, duplicate, ping-pong (the selection, then the same backwards), silence, a beat of silence, and the selection into the next empty slot. Loop: Set loop (saved as the slot's loop), play the loop round, crossfade its seam, and stretch it to a number of beats at the tempo, either keeping its pitch or by speed. Pitch: up or down a semitone or an octave with the length kept, or slower and faster tape-style. Tone: low and high cuts, mono, swap the sides, wider, trim the silence off the ends, and remove DC.

KeyLab Essential 61 mk3 (Arturia): it gets the MiniLab's layouts, plus its knob 9, faders 5–9 and the ‹ › by the display. In the Garage it runs the song: faders 1–8 are the volumes of eight tracks (the eight the selected track is among), fader 9 the song's master; knobs 1–6 the selected track's Smart Controls, 7 and 8 its pan and volume, 9 the song's tempo; ‹ › pick the track and the keys play it; the pads play its drums or chords. The transport keys drive the song: ▶ plays and stops it, ■ stops it (pressed again, back to the start), ● records on the selected track, the loop key turns Cycle on or off, ⏪ and ⏩ move a bar (⏪ at the start, or while the song plays, goes back to the start), Metro, Undo and Save do what they say. Shift + › opens the Garage from any screen, Shift + ‹ closes it. The transport keys reach Nate Bloom when the KeyLab's DAW program is set to MCU/HUI (on the KeyLab: Settings › DAW); in its Bitwig program they talk to Bitwig.

Live (top bar, or the menu on a phone): a screen for playing out, with no keys on it (the MiniLab has them). Eight knobs and four faders sit as they do on the MiniLab and move with it; drag one up or down to move it by touch. Knob pages: Sound (the sound's own knobs), FX, FX Lab (turning an effect up switches it on, all the way down switches it off), Mix and Mine (empty until you fill it). The faders stay put across pages: the sound's filter, Echo, Space and the master. Tap any knob's or fader's name to give it another job; Back to undoes it. Pad sets: Beat Pads banks A to D (Kit… loads a kit onto the bank), Notes and Chords in the key you've set, FX moves (hold a pad and the effect comes in, let go and it's back as it was), Samples and the Rhythm Creator's 16 tracks. On the MiniLab the big knob turns the knob pages and Shift + turn steps through the sounds (it plays the one you stop on); hold Shift and the knobs and faders switch to their Shift layer (the knobs: FX, or the page you tap while holding Shift; the faders: each sound that's playing and the beat); a click plays or stops what ▶ is set to (Seq, Rhythm or Pads), Shift + click records. FX opens every effect and the FX Lab at once. K.O. II sends it the clock and start/stop, so it plays in time.

Live on stage: the lock (next to Done) means Back, Esc or a stray tap can't close Live; hold Done to leave. The screen stays on while Live is open, and the thin meter by ■ lights when the safety limiter is catching peaks (nothing clips). Gear shows what's plugged in and who keeps time: K.O. II follows (Nate Bloom sends tempo, start and stop), K.O. II leads (its clock drives Nate Bloom and Live's ▶ target) or No sync; the clock goes only to the K.O. II when it's plugged in (the MIDI sheet's Clock buttons choose otherwise), so other gear never starts by surprise. Sample… records the K.O. II's USB audio (its firmware 2.5 or later) into the sampler. The set list (right of the knob pages) saves songs: the sound, tempo, FX and FX Lab, the Rhythm pattern, the Beat Pads bank and sequence and this screen's pages; ‹ › change song, on the next bar while something plays. With the MiniLab's big knob set to Songs, turning it cues a song and a click loads it. The column on the left shows what's playing, all the time: a card for each layer (lit: the one the knobs play; its Arp tag says whether it's arpeggiating, in the arp or left out, and a tap on it changes that; drag its bar for its level; ✕ stops it), Hold with the notes it's holding, the Arp, and the sounds themselves (Quick, Favourites or Lately: tap one to play it alone, + to layer it). Above them: the sound (‹ › step through, the layer count or + Layer a sound opens Sounds: what's playing, each layer in or out of the arp, Quick sounds, favourites and the ones played lately; tap a sound to play it alone, + to layer it), Hold, the Arp (tap to switch it on or off; hold it and it plays only while you hold) with its octaves, ⋯ for which layers arpeggiate, the pattern, speed and gate, and Split pads: the number keys 1–8 (top row or number pad) play pads 1–8 and the MiniLab's pads play 9–16. A computer keyboard runs Live too: number keys play pads, H is Hold, A the Arp (hold it down for only while held), Space ▶ / ■, and ? lists the rest; once a keyboard is used, each control shows its key. Sample (by the pad sets, or E) grabs the last 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars of what you heard, or records the K.O. II (or the mic), onto a pad of the Samples set (S1–S4 are its pads 13–16), kept after a reload. ● Rec records everything you hear, up to an hour, into this device's storage (a reload doesn't lose it); ▾ lists your recordings to save as WAV or MP3. ● Loop records into Beat Pads when ▶ plays Pads. CPU by the level meter shows how hard the tablet is working (the app's own work, whether the sound keeps up, and the audio engine's and the tablet's load where the browser reports them); a tap shows the breakdown and switches for the heaviest effects that are on. Log (or G) saves what Live was doing, the last minute of sound and every setting in one zip to send for a fix; its dot means the sound has fallen behind since the last log. Capture turns what you just played on the keys or the Notes and Chords pads (the last phrase, lined up to the bar of what's playing) into a new Garage track with that sound.

Record input (Smp tab): record from a USB audio interface, the mic or anything plugged in straight into the current slot, with a level meter and an option to chop it onto the pads. MIDI clock out (MIDI sheet) keeps hardware in time with the app.

Keep 90s: the last 90 seconds of everything you hear are always kept; tap Keep 90s and that moment becomes a take you can export as WAV or MP3, send to the sampler, or put on the Tracks. Nothing needs to be armed in advance.

Effects: one set of knobs (Drive, Wobble, Echo, Space) plus the FX Lab (Effects · FX Lab at the top of the FX tab, in Keys), applied only to the sounds you switch on in the Apply to row under the knobs: each synth, Drums, Sampler, Pads, Lib & Inst and DJ. Sounds that are off play clean, straight to the output. FX: On/Off switches every effect at once.

Effects per sound: the Effects panel edits the sound whose tab is open (the label says which): each synth, the drums, the sampler, the pads and the instruments keep their own Drive, Wobble, Echo, Space, on/off and amount, so a dry beat can sit under a washed-out pad. → All gives every sound the open sound's settings; Per sound / Global switches to one setting for everything. The Mixer's FX switch and the FX Lab stay global.

Garage: the Garage tab is a GarageBand-style studio, and Nate Bloom's one timeline: audio tracks record the microphone or Nate Bloom itself (everything you play, while the song plays), Import audio adds files, audio regions have Gain & fades, + Automation draws or records a plugin's control, a track's volume can be automated, Play along keeps the song going in a small bar, and → Garage (the take bar, the DJ, Beat Pads) puts audio there. What was in Tracks is a Garage song called Tracks. Choose an instrument (Keyboard, Drums, Smart Bass, Guitar and Strings, Synths, your plugins, the Sampler or the Audio Recorder) and it becomes a track; play it on the screen, a MIDI keyboard or the computer keyboard, press ● to record, then arrange the regions in Tracks, edit notes in the piano roll, mix, and export the song as WAV or MP3. ? in the Garage explains the rest.

Beats has two tabs at the top: Grid is the Rhythm Creator and Pads is Beat Pads (16 pads to play, record and chop samples onto; its ? explains them). Rhythm Creator (Grid): a 16-track drum machine: 808, 909, Analog, FM, Trap, hand percussion, Electro/FX and sample sounds, with accents, flams, rolls, step chance, nudges, per-track lengths, Euclidean fills, Make a beat, Vary, swing and Morph. Its ? button explains each part.

DJ: the DJ button in the top bar opens two decks. Load a song on each (a file, a sampler slot, or a bank pad), press AUTO SYNC and the other song is tempo-matched and beat-aligned to the lead; the sync dial and ◀◀ ▶▶ do the same by hand. Up to four songs sit in the crate, analysed for tempo, key and energy: queue one as next and MIX IN NEXT brings it in on the free deck (Blend, Bass swap, Cut, Filter or Echo out; Auto picks), or turn on Auto DJ and it mixes the next song in by itself as each one ends. Mix tempo (with a recommendation) and a suggested drum beat that plays in time are there too, and ● Rec records the whole mix. The 8-pad bank grabs exactly 1–16 beats from a deck or the mix, in time; hold a pad to send it to the sampler or the pads, or save a WAV. The ? button in the DJ view explains every control.

Juno Strings (168, after the Juno-106 and the KR-106): DCO mixes the oscillator the way the 106's sliders do — saw, saw + pulse, pulse, pulse + sub, sub + noise; Chorus is the 106's buttons, Off · I (slow, wide) · II (faster) · I+II (both, the thick one); HPF is its high-pass switch, off or three corners; PWM, Cutoff (with the filter envelope), Attack and Release as before.

Juno 106 (Sounds → browse → Plugins): the Ultramaster KR-106's engine — the Juno-60 and Juno-106 modelled from hardware measurements, firmware and schematics: the band-limited DCO, the IR3109 filter, the MN3009 bucket-brigade chorus (I, II, I+II), the real envelopes, a little component variance per voice. Six voices; the panel has the 106's front panel (LFO, DCO, HPF, VCF, envelope, VCA mode, chorus, Range, Assign: unison or poly, portamento, Model: 60 or 106) and the 256 factory patches of both machines as presets. It's the heaviest sound in the app (a real filter at twice the rate per voice), so on a phone keep it to one layer.

Twist (Sounds → browse → Plugins): Mutable Instruments' Plaits as an eight-voice instrument: pick a Model (24: analog with its filter, phase distortion, three banks of six-operator FM, wave terrain, string machine, chiptune, virtual analog, waveshaper, two-operator FM, formants, additive, wavetable, chords, speech, swarm, filtered noise, particles, plucked string, bells, bass drum, snare, hi-hat) and shape it with three knobs, Harmonics · Timbre · Morph, each doing something different per model. Decay and Colour are Plaits' low-pass gate (Colour: from a plain volume gate to a filter that closes with it); Attack and Release hold a note while the key is down; on the FM banks Harmonics picks the patch. 25 presets, one per model and a few more. The sound sits with your other sounds: layer it, give it effects, play it from the MiniLab.

Elephant in the FX Lab (Effects · FX Lab, after Shimmer): the same elephant as a general effect, on anything: every note you play sets the elephant's voice off at that note (a chord once, at its lowest), and what plays is vocoded onto it. Length is the shape's length in seconds; Blend from the mix as it is to the elephant alone with the mix's character; Glide, Wah, Growl, Drive, Level as in the sampler's mode. Live's FX Lab knob page has it too.

Galactic (Effects → Plugins): Airwindows' Galactic3, the huge, slowly drifting space that never quite ends. Replace is how much new sound takes the space over: low, and what's in there goes on and on (very low freezes it); high, and it's a normal long reverb. Detune drifts it, Brightness darkens the tail, Bigness is the room, Derez adds grit (and spares the CPU), Mix the balance. Put it on the whole mix, a send, or one sound.

Elephant (Smp tab, right of Grain): the trumpeting growl, with your sample's character on it. The elephant has a voice of its own (a saw with its octave and a sub) that the shape drives: the pitch glides up, two formants open with it (Wah), a saw-wave rasp grows in as it climbs (Growl), it's driven hard, and at the end it dives. The sample plays underneath with the same shape, and a vocoder puts the sample's spectrum onto the elephant's voice, so the result follows the elephant and still sounds like what you loaded; Blend is the balance, from the sample alone (with the shape on it) to the elephant alone (with the sample's character). Length is the shape's length: ×1 is the time the sample takes to play under the glide, so the dive lands on the sample's end, and a shorter sample gets a shorter elephant; stretch or squeeze it, or Fit to sample to put it back. dB trims the level (−24 to +12) so it sits against the other sounds. The pad under the sample bar moves Wah (left–right) and Growl (up–down) while you play, and the knobs follow; Shape… opens a sheet like the sample editor's, with the sample's waveform and the shape drawn over it (drag the amber end to set its length), the pad, a slider per control, Preview and Reset. The sample's wave is never changed: Play, Grain and Slice still have it as it was.

Fit a sample to the beat (Smp tab, under the slots): each slot's tempo is found automatically (½× / 2× if it's off). Match stretches it to the app's tempo, Fit 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 makes it exactly that many bars long. Length slows it down or speeds it up (¼× to 4×) without changing pitch; Pitch moves it ±12 semitones without changing length. It works while things play, the slot keeps its original, and Reset goes back to it. Loop (next to Play / Grain / Slice) keeps a held note looping.

Something not working? Tap Report a problem at the top of this help (☰ → Help) right after it happens. It saves one small zip (what you tapped, any errors and slow moments, every setting, device and audio details, and the last 30 seconds of sound) that you can send to get it fixed. Nothing is sent anywhere by itself.

In Lib, + next to any sound puts it on your tab bar, so it's always a tap away. Long-press a tab to remove it, hide an engine you don't use, move it, reset its sliders, rename it, or save the current settings as a sound of your own.

Playing from a keyboard (Bluetooth or USB) works like GarageBand's Musical Typing, here and in the Garage: A is C at the left edge of the keys, the home row A S D F G H J K L ; ' plays the white keys and W E T Y U O P the black keys. Z / X (or ↓ / ↑) move an octave down or up, C / V play softer or harder. On the drums A is the kick, S the snare, D the clap, F the hi-hat, H the open hat, J the tom, W the rim and E the cowbell; the 12 Pads and Beat Pads use the grid of keys that matches the pads on screen. Every key shows its letter. Two rows (the setting at the bottom of this help) gives the older layout instead: three octaves, the Z row and the Q row as the white keys. Space plays the beat, Esc stops, Shift+Enter is Hold, ← / → change the tempo, ? opens this help. Letters show on the keys once you've played from a keyboard:

Playing from a controller: how hard you hit a key sets the level (MIDI → Velocity: Off, Light touch, Normal or Firm); the pitch-bend and mod wheels move the ±2 and Vib wheels, aftertouch adds vibrato as you lean on a key, and the sustain pedal holds notes until you lift it. Plugin instruments get all of it. Hold keeps notes ringing after you let go, so you can play over them or arpeggiate them. Stop stops the beat and what you're playing in Keys, Seq and Notes; the Rhythm Creator, the DJ, Jam loops and Tracks keep going. The red ■ Stop all at the top (■ All on a phone, and in the DJ, Rhythm Creator and Tracks bars) stops everything. Play runs the sequencer and the Notes melody.

The button next to the tabs (it reads Single notes · Scale off to start) holds Chord (each key plays a chord), Scale (locks the keyboard to a key so nothing sounds wrong) and Blend (softens and overlaps notes). Mix (top) balances every sound, shows a level meter, and has the Effects switch and amount that bypass or scale every effect at once (also on the Effects panel).

Speech (the Speech tab) talks and sings, after Plogue's Chipspeech: thirteen voices from the machines that first spoke, from Bell Labs' Voder (1939) and the IBM 704 that sang Daisy Bell (1961) to the Speak & Spell, the Votrax and TI chips, SAM, DECtalk, MacInTalk and the Atari ST, each made the way its machine made it. Type the words; each note sings the next syllable (or the next word or line). A dash on its own holds a vowel over another note; [HH AH0 L OW1] gives exact phonemes. Speed shortens the consonants, Formant changes the size of the voice, Grit brings out the chip (past halfway it circuit-bends the Speak & Spell). Words come from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary.

Natewave (the Wave tab) is a wavetable synth in the spirit of Massive and Serum. Its eight faders are macros; Edit opens the rest: two oscillators that scan wavetables (a hundred built in, from analog shapes and vowels to FM and phase distortion, or one made from your own sample), each with a warp (bend, sync, mirror, squeeze, crush, FM, fold, ring), unison and width; a sub and noise; two filters (ladder, state-variable, comb, formant) in series, parallel or split; three envelopes; three LFOs that can lock to the tempo. To modulate anything, drag a source from the strip at the bottom onto a knob (or tap the source, then knobs) and drag the knob to set how much; the Mod matrix lists every line and can scale one by another (a macro that sets how much an LFO moves). Mutate changes the sound a little, more or a lot, keeping the sections you lock; ↶ goes back. Make a sound (Osc → wavetable → Yours) turns a sample into a wavetable and copies how it starts, fades and darkens into the envelopes and filter. Natewave's sounds are in the Sound browser under Natewave.

Your plugins (YOURS in the plugin list): Twin 77, Duet 80, Nebula 80, Hypnagogia, Para Four, Stab 85, Pop 89, Unicorn Dream, Fuzz Bass and the Ballad Kit 88 drum kit, and the Glitch Starter effect, built from your own CLAP and nih-plug code, so they sound as they do in Bitwig. They follow Nate Bloom's tempo; Glitch Starter glitches while the sequencer plays, and plays C, D, E, F, G as stutter, reverse, tape stop, crush, gate.

Playing sounds together: the Playing row (Synths; it appears once two or more sounds play together) shows every sound the keys play, and the sequencer and MIDI play them all too. Under it, Quick has the engines and the sounds you keep there: tap one to play it alone, + to play it with what's playing, ✓ to stop it; ✎ chooses what's in the row (your sounds, which engines show, + any sound from the list). The + on a Quick sound, or + Together next to any sound in the sound list or in Plugins, adds one: Nate Bloom's synth engines, Studio instruments, library sounds, plugins (yours too), your SF2/SFZ instrument and a Sampler slot, in any mix. Tap a sound in the row to see its controls; ✕ stops that one; Only … keeps just the highlighted one; ⚙ opens a plugin's own panel. Tapping a sound in the sound list (or Play alone in Plugins) plays it by itself and stops the rest; the ‹ › arrows swap the highlighted one. Each has its own fader in Mix, and picking a plugin never plays a note by itself.

⚡ Fast plugins (in the apps, at the top of Plugins): your own plugins (YOURS) play from the app's low-latency audio, the way the fast pads do, so they answer sooner when you play them live. While it's on, Nate Bloom's effects, Takes and Tracks don't hear them; turn it off to record them.

Link (in the apps): the tablet app and the Linux app on the same Wi-Fi. On the computer, Link → Turn on. On the tablet, ☰ → Link → Find computers, pick it and type the code the computer shows (only the first time). Then send this project either way, and with Play together on, both share one tempo and Play and Stop start and stop both at the same moment.

Ableton Link (in the apps, under Link): Nate Bloom joins the Link session on your network, with Bitwig, Live and the other Link apps. The tempo follows both ways, the sequencer and the Rhythm Creator stay on the session's beats, and Play waits for the next bar so it starts on the beat with the others (if they're already playing, it comes in on the next 16th). With Start/Stop Sync on, Play and Stop here start and stop them too, and theirs start and stop Nate Bloom. In Bitwig: Settings → Synchronization → Sync Method: Link, then the Link button by the transport.

Studio (in the sound browser) has real recorded instruments that download the first time you pick one: pianos (the grand also with 3 velocity layers, and an upright), strings, double bass, harp, mallets, brass, woodwinds and a pipe organ. Instruments with velocity layers play a softer recording, not just a quieter one, when you play gently on a MIDI keyboard or lower a note's velocity in the piano roll.

Plugin controls: in MIDI learn, tap Plugin control… to pick any loaded plugin's control, then move a knob on your controller. In the Garage, + Automation adds a lane for a plugin control: draw its moves with your finger, or press ● on the lane and turn the control (in the plugin's panel or with a learned knob) while Tracks plays; ● records over what's there, so turn it off to hear the lane play back. A on the lane turns it off and on; ✕ removes it (↶ brings it back).

Undo and redo: ↶ in the top bar (or Ctrl+Z; Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes) takes back the last change wherever it was: the sound (knobs, presets, engines, effects), Notes, the Seq pattern, the Rhythm Creator, Beat Pads and the Garage. The full screens (Beats and the Garage) have the same ↶ in their top bar, for the changes you made there. History (☰) lists the changes, newest first, with Undo and Redo; tap one to go back to just before it.

Piano roll (Notes → Roll): the same melody as the staff on a grid of pitches and steps. Tap to add a note of the chosen length, drag a note to move it, drag its right end to change its length, tap it to erase it. The lane underneath sets velocity: drag a note's bar (the note you last touched, or the whole chord that starts there). Drag empty space to scroll. ↶ undoes moves, lengths and velocity too.

Sound out (in MIDI, on a computer, a Chromebook or the Linux app): pick the output Nate Bloom plays through, such as a USB audio interface, headphones or HDMI. The Linux app's fast pads follow it. On the tablet, Android decides (the last thing plugged in).

Plugins (☰): Web Audio Modules, the web's plugin format, so they run on the iPhone, Android, Chromebook and Linux alike. Instruments (Nate Bloom's physical models: Marimba, Nylon Guitar, Violin, Flute, Clarinet and Voice; plus Synth-101, Spectrum: Modal and the DRM-16 drum sampler) are sounds: they're under Plugins in the sound browser and in Play together, and the keys, sequencer, arp, chords and MIDI play them. Effects (Nate Bloom's Hall, Plate, Cloud, Tape Echo, Ensemble, Juno Chorus, Tape, Lo-Fi, Ladder Filter, Bus Compressor, Auto-Pan and Ring Mod, plus amps, fuzz, phasers, EQ and more; each of Nate Bloom's has presets) run on the whole mix, after Nate Bloom's own effects; turn each one on or off, move it up the chain, or remove it. Controls opens a plugin's own panel; its settings are kept. Add by link loads any other Web Audio Module from the address of its index.js. Effects go on the Whole mix, on any one sound (pick it in the row above the list: each synth, Drums, Library, Beat Pads, a plugin instrument…), or on Send A and Send B: shared effects that every sound feeds at its own Send level, the way a studio shares one reverb. Plugins are in everything you record: takes, Keep 90s, Tracks and stems (the sends are stems of their own). Fast pads pause while a plugin effect is on the mix or on Beat Pads.

VST3, CLAP and LV2 (the Linux app): the plugins on your computer, in ~/.vst3, ~/.clap and ~/.lv2, the system's plugin folders (/usr/lib/vst3, /usr/lib/clap, /usr/lib/lv2 and the like) and any folder you add, are listed first in Plugins and under Plugins in the sound browser. Their instruments play alone or together with what's playing, from the keys, chords, the arp, the sequencer and MIDI; their effects go wherever Nate Bloom's do (the whole mix, a sound, a send, another plugin). Controls opens the plugin's own window beside Nate Bloom's (a plugin built without one, as nih-plug builds them unless an editor is added, or one whose window won't open, shows its parameters as sliders in the panel instead); what you change there is kept with the plugin, and MIDI learn and Tracks automation reach its parameters. Each plugin works outside the browser's one audio thread, and its sound comes back through Nate Bloom's mixer, effects and recording. An effect plays about 8 ms after what goes into it (more if a plugin needs it). Look again finds new ones. Plugins run inside Nate Bloom, so one that crashes takes Nate Bloom down with it. When that happens, the plugins that were loaded don't load by themselves the next time, and Load anyway lets each one back in. Plugins need Linux builds (x86-64); Windows and Mac ones won't load. An LV2 plugin's own window opens when it's made for X11 (most are); one made for another toolkit (GTK, Qt) has its controls under MIDI learn and automation instead.

Your inputs through plugins (the Linux app, in Plugins): an input of your audio interface (the Volt 2's 1, 2 or both: a guitar, a mic, a synth) played live into a channel of its own, Input, with its own level. Plugin effects go on it from the Input tab above the effect list (an amp sim, a reverb), Nate Bloom's own effects too, and it's in everything you record. The panel says how long after you play you hear it; turn the interface's direct monitor off so you don't hear it twice.

⚙ Settings and Find (on the strip): Settings is the rig in one place: Sound check (where the sound goes, volume, buffer, dropouts, a test tone), MIDI, Gear, Pixelbook, Projects, App and this Help. Find (also the / key or Ctrl-K) searches everything: type two letters and get settings, sounds, songs, pad sets, help and actions such as FX off or Rescan MIDI; Enter runs the first one. The strip across the top shows the tempo, key and bar, dots for the MiniLab, the K.O. II and the Pixelbook, where the sound goes, dropouts, Log and Find, and the three spaces: Play (the performance surface), Build (Sounds · Beats · Rhythm · Samples · Songs · Tracks · DJ, with one transport, the Sounds browser and the Mixer as drawers) and ⚙ Settings.

☰ (top right) holds Settings, Find, Live, Projects, Plugins, My sounds, Export, MIDI, Gear (what's plugged in and the sound buffer), Link, Install, History, Help and Exit. The Motion tab (Sound · FX · Arp · Motion · Jam) morphs between snapshots and has Mutate and macros; LFOs · XY there opens the two LFOs, the XY pad and drift. ▾ at the end of the tabs hides the sliders.

Tap the bpm readout in time to set a tempo. ♩ turns on a click. Drummer (Seq view) makes any beat change by itself: fills, breakdowns and half-time in 16-bar phrases. Song (in Seq, next to Beats…) chains your own patterns A–D into an arrangement.

No sound after switching apps or plugging in headphones? reopens the sound on whatever is plugged in now. It also happens by itself when you come back to the app or plug something in.
